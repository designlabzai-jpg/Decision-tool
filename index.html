How to use FlipLog

A practical guide — read this once and you'll know everything you need.

1
Who is this for?

This tool is for anyone who buys and resells items — on eBay, Facebook Marketplace, car boot sales, Vinted, or any other platform. You don't need to be a professional. If you've ever thought "was that a good deal?" or "should I buy this?", this tool is for you.

Examples: resellers who flip second-hand goods, collectors tracking what their purchases are worth, side-hustlers testing whether flipping is profitable.

2
What does it do?

It helps you make better buy/pass decisions by tracking three things:

  • Before you decide: Is this a strong opportunity or a weak one? The app rates it based on your inputs.
  • After you decide: What actually happened? Did you profit, miss out, or make a bad call?
  • Over time: Are you improving? The Insights section shows patterns across all your decisions.
3
Adding a deal

When you spot a listing you're considering, add it straight away. Fill in:

  • Item name — describe it clearly so you recognise it later
  • Listing price — what the seller is asking
  • Market value estimate — what you think it would sell for (be honest)
  • Costs — platform fee (default 12%), shipping, other costs. The app calculates net upside automatically.
  • Buy trigger (optional) — a price at which you'd definitely buy. When you edit the deal later and the listing price hits that number, the card highlights green. Useful for deals you're watching.
  • Notes (optional) — condition, seller context, anything relevant.

Example: listing at €180, market value €240, 12% fee + €8 shipping = costs €29.60. Net upside = €30.40.

4
Decision quality (optional but important)

Tap "Decision quality" to open the advanced section. This tells the app how confident you actually are in your estimate. By default all fields are set to cautious values — the app will label most deals "Needs verification" until you fill this in.

  • Market value source — gut feeling, one sale found, 3+ comparable sales, or expert source
  • Comparable quality — same item and condition, similar item, or weak comparison
  • Listing risk — how risky does the listing feel (unclear photos, new seller = high)
  • Condition / authenticity clarity — do you know exactly what you're getting?

The app only shows Strong estimated edge when all four are strong AND the net upside is above your minimum threshold. Otherwise it shows Needs verification. Each button shows "Default" or "Refined" so you always know what you've changed.

5
Understanding the deal card

Each deal card shows you several things at a glance:

  • Edge label — Strong estimated edge / Needs verification / No edge
  • Missing signals — exactly why a deal is not strong edge (e.g. "Risk too high", "Not enough comparable sales")
  • Recommended action — one concrete next step based on the top failure signal
  • Quality indicator — whether you used default assumptions, partially refined, or high-quality inputs
  • Signal grid — four coloured dots showing source, comparable quality, risk, and clarity at a glance
  • Buy trigger flag — green banner if the listing price is at or below your target
6
Buy or Pass

Once you've decided, tap Buy or Pass. The app records your decision and the timestamp. You can undo if you change your mind before recording an outcome. The app never tells you what to do before you decide — it only shows estimated opportunity.

7
Recording what happened

After you've acted, come back and record the outcome. The options differ depending on your decision:

  • If you bought: "Sold — enter resale price" (confirms real profit/loss), "Still holding it", "Sold — no price data", or "Unknown"
  • If you passed: "Listing sold — enter price" (checks your call), "Still listed", "No longer visible", or "Unknown"

When entering a sold price after a pass, also select the reason (poor listing, seller urgency, condition issue, etc.) — this affects how the verdict is scored. A pass due to a real condition concern is treated differently from one where you just missed an undervalued deal.

Outcomes marked "Still holding" or "Still listed" show a prompt after 7 days reminding you to update them.

The app never presents guesses as confirmed facts. Estimated and confirmed data are always clearly labelled separately.

8
Managing your deals
  • Edit — update any field on a deal at any time (price, market value, costs, trigger, notes)
  • Duplicate — copy a deal to use as a starting point for a similar listing
  • Search — type in the search box to filter deals by name instantly
  • Sort — sort by date added, net upside, or listing price
  • Filter — show only strong edge, needs verification, undecided, or decided deals
  • Pages — deals are shown 9 per page so the list never gets overwhelming
  • Bulk update — check multiple decided deals and mark them all as "No longer visible", "Still listed", or "Unknown" at once
9
Reading your insights

The Insights section (bottom of the Tracker tab) shows patterns after a few completed decisions. You'll see:

  • A summary grid — total decisions, buys, passes, confirmed P&L, estimated missed upside, needs review
  • A profit-over-time chart (confirmed buys only)
  • A decision breakdown bar chart
  • A confidence vs outcome comparison
  • Up to 3 detected patterns — e.g. "You often accept small wins", "Your high-confidence deals perform better"

Confirmed P&L only ever counts deals with a real resale price. Estimated figures are always labelled as such. The two are never mixed.

You can also set your minimum worthwhile profit in the Insights section. This changes what counts as a "Good buy" vs a "Small win" across the whole app.

10
Backing up and restoring your data

Your data is saved in your browser only. If you clear your browser data or switch devices, it is gone. Three buttons sit below the decision history:

  • Backup JSON — saves all deals, decisions, outcomes, and your threshold setting to a file
  • Export CSV — exports a spreadsheet you can open in Excel or Google Sheets
  • Import JSON — restores from a backup file. Merges with existing deals — duplicates are skipped automatically

Back up regularly. Once browser data is cleared, it cannot be recovered.

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions most users have.

Is my data safe? Can anyone else see it?
Yes. Your data is stored only in your own browser's local storage — on your device, not on any server. This is true whether you use the hosted version on GitHub Pages or a downloaded file. GitHub cannot see your deals. Nobody can. The downside is that if you clear your browser data or switch devices, it is gone. Use Backup JSON regularly and keep the file somewhere safe.
What is the difference between the hosted version and downloading the file?
They are the same app. The hosted version at designlabzai-jpg.github.io/Decision-tool runs directly in your browser without needing to download anything — easier to share and open on any device. The downloaded HTML file works the same way but offline. Your data stays in your browser either way and is never sent anywhere. One thing to note: if you use the hosted version on a different browser or device than usual, your saved deals will not be there — they are tied to the specific browser you used.
Why does the app never say "this is a good deal" before I decide?
Because it cannot know. Your market value estimate might be wrong, the item might have hidden flaws, or the seller might not be trustworthy. The app shows estimated opportunity — strong or weak — but the final judgment is always yours. It only evaluates your decision after you record what actually happened.
What is "net upside" and why is it different from raw profit?
Raw upside is market value minus listing price. Net upside is what's left after subtracting platform fee, shipping, and other costs. A deal with €60 raw upside might only have €30 net — that's what you'd actually keep. The app always uses net upside for decisions and edge classification.
What does "Needs verification" mean?
It means the deal has positive net upside but the app isn't confident enough to call it a strong opportunity. One or more quality signals are weak — for example, your estimate is based on a gut feeling, or the condition is unclear. The card shows exactly which signals are missing. Open the Decision quality section, improve the inputs, and it may upgrade.
What is "Strong estimated edge"?
The app's highest pre-decision rating. All of these must be true: net upside is at or above your minimum threshold, market estimate is based on 3+ comparable sales or an expert source, comparison is same item and condition, risk is low or medium, condition/authenticity is clear, and you intentionally filled in the Decision quality section. It still says "estimated" because nothing is confirmed until you buy and resell.
What are "Missing signals" on a card?
These are the specific reasons why a deal is not classified as Strong estimated edge — for example "Risk too high" or "Not enough comparable sales". They map directly to the Decision quality inputs. Fix the underlying issue (do more research, verify condition) or update your inputs if you've done that work.
What is the "Recommended action"?
A single suggested next step based on the most important missing signal. Examples: "Ask for better proof, photos, or verification", "Find more comparable outcomes before deciding", "Skip — no upside after costs". It's a prompt, not a command — the decision is always yours.
What is the buy trigger?
A price you set on a deal — if the listing price drops to that level, the card shows a green flag. The app doesn't monitor prices automatically. You update the listing price manually (via Edit) when you check the listing, and the flag appears instantly if it's hit your target. Useful for deals you're watching but haven't acted on yet.
What is the minimum profit threshold?
A number you set in the Insights section (default €50). It defines what counts as a worthwhile profit. Confirmed buys below this are labelled "Small win" rather than "Good buy". It also affects edge classification — a deal with net upside below your threshold will never be Strong estimated edge. Change it any time to match your standards.
What is "Outcome conflict"?
This appears when the final sold price is very different from your market value estimate — more than 40% lower or higher than expected. The app refuses to score this as a simple good or bad call because something unusual likely happened. It might mean your estimate was off, or the item sold for a non-market reason (damaged, urgent seller, wrong category listing). Review before drawing conclusions.
I passed on a deal and it sold above the listing price. Is that always a missed opportunity?
Not necessarily. If you recorded a reason like "condition issue" or "authenticity concern", the verdict softens to "Pass may have been reasonable" — the risk was real even if the sale went through. All other reasons (poor listing, seller urgency, etc.) result in "Missed opportunity" since the upside was there and you passed.
Why does the outcome section look different for Buy vs Pass?
Because the questions are different. After a Buy, you record your resale price — the app confirms your actual profit or loss. After a Pass, you record what the listing sold for (if you know) — the app checks whether your decision was correct. The framing, labels, and fields all reflect which side of the decision you were on.
What happens to "Still holding" or "Still listed" deals?
They are not counted as good or bad — just paused. After 7 days a prompt appears on the card reminding you to check and update the outcome. Use the bulk selection feature to update multiple stale deals at once.
Can I use this for things other than eBay?
Yes. The app is platform-neutral. It works for any buying and reselling context — Facebook Marketplace, Vinted, local auctions, car boot sales, trading cards, electronics, clothing. The "Platform fee %" field covers any selling commission regardless of where you sell.
Why don't prices update automatically?
The app has no connection to any platform or API. All prices are entered manually. This keeps it simple, private, and free. The buy trigger feature is the closest equivalent — it flags when you manually update a price to your target level.
I have a lot of old deals with no outcome. What should I do?
Use bulk selection. Check the boxes on multiple decided cards, then use the action bar that appears at the bottom to mark them all at once — "No longer visible", "Still listed", or "Unknown". This cleans up your tracker and makes the Insights section more accurate.
How do I move my data to another device?
Click "Backup JSON" to download your data. Open the app on the new device, click "Import JSON", and select that file. All deals, decisions, and outcomes are restored. Duplicates are automatically skipped if you import the same file twice.
What does "Confirmed P&L" mean in the metrics?
Confirmed profit and loss — calculated only from buy decisions where you've entered a real resale price. It never includes estimates or guesses. "Estimated missed upside" is shown separately and always labelled as estimated. The two are never added together.
The deals are paginated — how many show per page?
9 deals per page (3 rows of 3). Use the page buttons below the grid to navigate. The page resets to 1 whenever you change a filter, sort order, or search term. Clicking a page number scrolls back to the top of the grid automatically.
